
### Judging Criteria

Your project will be scored from 1-5 points in each category:

1. Innovation
   * 5 points: Novel solution with clear advantages over existing options
   * 3 points: Good improvement on existing solutions
   * 1 point: Basic implementation of existing idea
2. Curiosity
   * 5 points: Deep exploration of problem space, multiple approaches tested
   * 3 points: Solid research and testing of one approach
   * 1 point: Minimal exploration of alternatives
3. Communication
   * 5 points: Crystal clear presentation with excellent visuals
   * 3 points: Clear explanation with basic visuals
   * 1 point: Unclear presentation, minimal visual aids
4. Project Breadth & Complexity
   * 5 points: Integrates multiple technologies/domains expertly
   * 3 points: Good use of one or two technologies
   * 1 point: Very basic technical implementation
5. Impact
   * 5 points: Clear, feasible solution to significant problem
   * 3 points: Addresses a real need with reasonable solution
   * 1 point: Limited practical application
6. Challenge Navigation
   * 5 points: Overcame major obstacles with creative solutions
   * 3 points: Handled basic challenges effectively
   * 1 point: Limited problem-solving demonstrated

## 1. Global Rubric Level Definitions

Projects are graded across four overarching mastery levels, corresponding to standard grading scales:

| Rubric Level | Definition | Suggested Point Allocation / Grade |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Inadequate** | The learning objective has not been met at all. | 0% grade |
| **Developing** | Some portion of the learning objective has been met or attempted; but not at an acceptable level. | 50% grade or non-passing grade |
| **Emerging / Intermediate** | Most of the learning objective has been met at an acceptable level. | 70–80% grade, or B/C passing grade |
| **Accomplished / Expert** | The learning objective has been met in its entirety. | 100% or A grade |

## 2. Pillar 1: Curiosity (Innovation & Questioning)

> *“A curious disposition opens doors to continuous learning, new perspectives, and boundless growth opportunities. It positions us to explore and be open to new opportunities. Curiosity often manifests in hackathon projects through innovative problem-solving approaches, unconventional ideas, diverse technologies, and a relentless pursuit of creative solutions to complex challenges.”*

### Criteria 1.1: Innovation (EMLO 2: Analyze Accepted Solutions)
* **Definition:** Challenge the norm by exploring weaknesses in existing solutions through testing or research, then exploring ways to improve or reinvent.
* **Expanded EMLO:** Explore a contrarian view of currently accepted products, processes, and services.
* **Key Interview Question:** *"How did your team challenge commonly accepted ideas and what did you do differently in your project?"*
* **What Good Looks Like:** A project that improves an existing solution in a way that increases value (access, affordability, opportunity, empowerment), or a brand-new approach addressing an unexamined problem. Both cases involve a team identifying a problem and examining existing solutions.

#### Sub-Criteria & Behavioral Indicators:
* **a. Determine possible gaps or flaws in a currently accepted solution using direct testing or by conducting primary or secondary research:**
  * *Accomplished (5 pts):* Identified possible gaps or flaws (tangible fixes) in a currently accepted solution supported by evidence (direct experimentation or research).
  * *Advanced (4 pts):* Identified possible gaps or flaws in a currently accepted solution but did not conduct adequate research.
  * *Intermediate (3 pts):* Identified possible gaps or flaws in a currently accepted solution without conducting any research.
  * *Developing/Inadequate (1–2 pts):* Did not identify possible gaps or flaws and did not conduct research.
* **b. Conduct primary or secondary research to determine the strengths and weaknesses of a currently accepted solution to a problem:**
  * *Accomplished (5 pts):* Conducted primary or secondary research to determine strengths and weaknesses.
  * *Intermediate (3 pts):* Identified strengths and weaknesses without conducting primary/secondary research; or conducted research without identifying adequate strengths/weaknesses.
  * *Developing/Inadequate (1–2 pts):* Did not conduct research and did not identify strengths or weaknesses.
* **c. Consider a potential improvement that could be made to a currently accepted solution to a problem:**
  * *Accomplished (5 pts):* The identified improvement to a currently accepted solution to a problem was considered and explored.
  * *Intermediate (3 pts):* The change identified does not improve the currently accepted solution.
  * *Developing/Inadequate (1–2 pts):* No possible improvement was considered.

### Criteria 1.2: Creativity / Questioning (EMLO 1: Demonstrate Curiosity)
* **Definition:** Being curious, ask questions that inspire growth and result in the artful application of scientific principles.
* **Expanded EMLO:** Ask and encourage questions that facilitate and inspire growth and learning.
* **Key Interview Question:** *"Can you share some examples of questions your team asked while exploring possibilities? Show us something unique, beautiful, or clever in your project."*
* **What Good Looks Like:** A team that questioned existing limitations, sought to discover an unaddressed problem, or built an out-of-the-box project leveraging unique ideas/processes.

#### Sub-Criteria & Behavioral Indicators:
* **a. Engage in structured group inquiry:**
  * *Accomplished (5 pts):* Engaged in structured group inquiry, including asking questions that facilitate and inspire growth and learning; acted on the plan to create project outcomes artfully.
  * *Advanced (4 pts):* Engaged in structured group inquiry, made a plan exploring answers to questions to improve project outcomes.
  * *Intermediate (3 pts):* Engaged in structured group inquiry, but questions did not fully facilitate growth and learning.
  * *Developing/Inadequate (1–2 pts):* Attended group inquiry sessions but did not engage or did not engage in group inquiry.
* **c. Formulate questions that solicit new knowledge:**
  * *Accomplished (5 pts):* New knowledge can be solicited from formulated questions; team asked meaningful questions and discovered answers that improved understanding.
  * *Intermediate (3 pts):* Some formulated questions solicit new knowledge, but not all.
  * *Developing/Inadequate (1–2 pts):* Questions are formulated but do not solicit new knowledge, or no questions are formulated.

## 3. Pillar 2: Connections (Communication, Complexity & Resources)

> *“Connecting diverse sets of information and skills unlocks the potential for groundbreaking innovation by fostering the cross-pollination of ideas and the synthesis of novel solutions.”*

### Criteria 2.1: Communication & Visual Representations (EMLO 9: Develop Concepts & Visual Representations)
* **Definition:** Effectively communicating project concepts including a visual representation that explains the project's development process and purpose.
* **Expanded EMLO:** Represent and refine conceptual solutions through the use of visual representations.
* **Key Interview Question:** *"What part of your presentation/video shows off your work best? Can you show me your prototype and how it explains your concept?"*
* **What Good Looks Like:** Seen in team project videos, slide decks, physical prototypes, posters, or diagrams describing the project and process.

#### Sub-Criteria & Behavioral Indicators:
* **a. Develop a detailed representation of a conceptual solution:**
  * *Accomplished (5 pts):* Detailed representation of a conceptual solution was developed via a functioning prototype of essential elements based on research and iterative design (sketches, drawings, diagrams).
  * *Advanced (4 pts):* Prototype of essential elements along with sketches and diagrams.
  * *Intermediate (3 pts):* Representation of a conceptual solution was developed but not fully detailed / representation of essential elements without additional documentation.
  * *Developing/Inadequate (1–2 pts):* Representation was incomplete or no representation developed.
* **b. Illustrate preliminary conceptual solutions through visual representations:**
  * *Accomplished (5 pts):* Preliminary conceptual solutions were illustrated using visual representations (physical prototype and visual explanation).
  * *Intermediate (3 pts):* Visual representations of preliminary solutions were incomplete or did not use visual representations.
* **c. Explain the value of modeling conceptual solutions through visual representations:**
  * *Accomplished (5 pts):* Value of modeling conceptual solutions through visual representations is explained.
  * *Intermediate (3 pts):* Visual representations identified but their value is not fully explained.
  * *Developing/Inadequate (1–2 pts):* No explanation given.

---

### Criteria 2.2: Project Breadth & Technical Complexity (EMLO 3: Integrate Information through Making Connections)
* **Definition:** Leveraging knowledge and skills from the humanities, engineering, and science to successfully make complex/nontrivial new innovations.
* **Expanded EMLO:** Make connections between different domains of knowledge to reach new and innovative ideas and solutions.
* **Key Interview Question:** *"How did you use information from two different areas to explore a new idea? What was the most complicated piece of this project to build? What could this project look like if it was 10X as big?"*
* **What Good Looks Like:** Use of diverse programming languages, frameworks, APIs, data, or perspectives. Teams may use resources from different domains like Medicine + Robotics or Environmental Sciences + Economics, considering future scalability.

#### Sub-Criteria & Behavioral Indicators:
* **a. Synthesize ideas from a wide variety of sources to discover something new:**
  * *Accomplished (5 pts):* Ideas were fully synthesized using a wide variety of sources across humanities, science, and engineering to create something new, future-oriented, and effectively complex.
  * *Advanced (4 pts):* Connect ideas from more than 2 sources, across different areas of humanities, engineering, and science.
  * *Intermediate (3 pts):* Ideas from a variety of sources were partially synthesized / connected ideas from more than one technical area.
  * *Developing/Inadequate (1–2 pts):* Ideas came from a limited variety of sources without synthesis, or a single idea from a single domain.
* **b. Connect ideas from more than one domain of knowledge:**
  * *Accomplished (5 pts):* Ideas were fully connected with more than one domain of knowledge.
  * *Intermediate (3 pts):* Ideas were partially connected with more than one domain.
  * *Developing/Inadequate (1–2 pts):* Ideas were connected within only one domain or not connected.
* **c. Connect ideas from the same domain of knowledge:**
  * *Accomplished (5 pts):* Ideas were fully connected within the same domain.
  * *Developing/Inadequate (1–2 pts):* Ideas were brainstormed but not connected.

---

### Criteria 2.3: Resources & Expertise (EMLO 13: Identify and Utilize Resources and Expertise)
* **Definition:** Identify gaps in knowledge, resources that could fill that gap, and how those resources can be used to advance a solution.
* **Key Interview Question:** *"What helpful feedback did you get from a mentor or what other source of expertise did you tap into?"*

#### Sub-Criteria & Behavioral Indicators:
* **a. Use resources and expertise to advance an element of a solution:**
  * *Accomplished (5 pts):* Resources and expertise were clearly used to advance an element of a solution.
  * *Developing/Inadequate (1–2 pts):* No resources or expertise were used.
* **b. Identify needed resources or expertise to fill an identified knowledge gap:**
  * *Accomplished (5 pts):* Resources and expertise were fully identified to fill a knowledge gap.
  * *Developing/Inadequate (1–2 pts):* No resources or expertise identified.
* **c. Identify knowledge gap related to a specific topic:**
  * *Accomplished (5 pts):* Knowledge gap identified was related to a specific topic.
  * *Developing/Inadequate (1–2 pts):* No knowledge gap was identified.

## 4. Pillar 3: Creating Value (Impact, Solution Design, Failure & Validation)

> *“Opportunities often lie in the intersection of failure and persistence. Hackathon projects that aim to create value and embrace setbacks as steppingstones lead to extraordinary innovations. Does this project address the right problem? Does it consider what stakeholders hold most valuable and has the team applied lessons learned from a setback or mistake?”*

### Criteria 3.1: Impact (EMLO 5: Identify Opportunity to Create Value)
* **Definition:** The project seeks to provide a solution with potential social, economic/business, environmental, or other tangible value.
* **Expanded EMLO:** Create ideas for new products, processes, or services that provide a potential social, economic, or environmental value.
* **Key Interview Question:** *"What does your project improve or who might it help? What user needs did you keep in mind while working on this? Did you get any feedback from your target audience?"*
* **What Good Looks Like:** Projects with well-defined user needs, research-backed justification, and consideration of reach, adoption, and long-term implementation effects.

#### Sub-Criteria & Behavioral Indicators:
* **a. Propose an opportunity to create a product, process, or service and justify that it can be developed to create value using research from multiple sources:**
  * *Accomplished (5 pts):* Proposed opportunity creates value AND can be developed; team identified an opportunity for impact and justified their concept can be developed to create value through their prototype and research from multiple sources.
  * *Advanced (4 pts):* Proposed opportunity creates value and can be developed, but lacks multi-source research backup (or feasible development).
  * *Intermediate (3 pts):* Proposed opportunity can be developed but does not create value, OR creates value but cannot be developed; features refined using research.
  * *Developing/Inadequate (1–2 pts):* Proposed opportunity does not create value AND cannot be developed; no opportunity proposed.
* **b. Given a broad description of an opportunity, refine the opportunity based on research:**
  * *Accomplished (5 pts):* Description is modified AND fully supported by research.
  * *Intermediate (3 pts):* Description is modified but only partially supported by research.
  * *Developing/Inadequate (1–2 pts):* Description is modified but not based on research, or original description used without refinement.
* **c. Describe the features of an identified opportunity:**
  * *Accomplished (5 pts):* All features are identified AND all descriptions are complete.
  * *Intermediate (3 pts):* All features identified and most descriptions given, but not all complete.
  * *Developing/Inadequate (1–2 pts):* Features identified but not described, or no features identified.

---

### Criteria 3.2: Solution Design & Requirements Analysis (EMLO 10: Analyze Solutions & Develop Design Requirements)
* **Definition:** Select a final concept solution based on user needs and student-generated design requirements.

#### Sub-Criteria & Behavioral Indicators:
* **a. Analyze proposed concepts based on user needs and student-generated design requirements:**
  * *Accomplished (5 pts):* Proposed concepts were analyzed through judging them against user needs and student-generated design requirements.
  * *Intermediate (3 pts):* Analyzed against either user needs or student-generated design requirements, but not both.
  * *Developing/Inadequate (1–2 pts):* Analyzed without judging against user needs/requirements or no analysis provided.
* **b. Select the best solution from the proposed concepts and provided design requirements:**
  * *Accomplished (5 pts):* Best solution was selected from proposed concepts and provided design requirements.
  * *Developing/Inadequate (1–2 pts):* Solution selected was not from proposed concepts/requirements or no solution selected.
* **c. Describe the process of analyzing potential solutions and determining design requirements:**
  * *Accomplished (5 pts):* Process of analyzing potential solutions and determining design requirements is fully described.
  * *Intermediate (3 pts):* Processes are only partially described.
  * *Developing/Inadequate (1–2 pts):* Only one process described or process not described.

---

### Criteria 3.3: Growth / Navigating Challenges (EMLO 6: Learn from Failure)
* **Definition:** Testing ideas, recognizing barriers or failures as important moments of feedback, and pivoting accordingly.
* **Expanded EMLO:** Persist through and learn from failure.
* **Key Interview Question:** *"Name one thing that you struggled with or something that just didn't work at all and how did you pivot your plan from there? How did you test your ideas and make changes based on the results?"*
* **What Good Looks Like:** Teams who chose a crucial pivot in their project due to a knowledge gap or a team that made changes to their project after testing it and discovering a problem.

#### Sub-Criteria & Behavioral Indicators:
* **a. Consistently record and reflect on personal or team failures and identify successful solutions in areas that had previously been failures:**
  * *Accomplished (5 pts):* Consistent recording and reflection on failures resulted in successful solutions, including lessons learned from previous failures throughout the solution.
  * *Advanced (4 pts):* Consistent recording and reflection resulted in successful solutions, but did not explicitly identify lessons learned.
  * *Intermediate (3 pts):* Recordings and reflections did not identify successful solutions or lessons learned.
  * *Developing/Inadequate (1–2 pts):* Failures were not recorded or reflected on.
* **b. Reflect on a previous personal or team failure, and use lessons learned from that failure to improve a solution:**
  * *Accomplished (5 pts):* Reflection identified lessons learned and used them to improve a solution.
  * *Intermediate (3 pts):* Reflection identified lessons learned but were not used to improve a solution.
  * *Developing/Inadequate (1–2 pts):* Reflection did not identify any lessons learned or no reflection provided.
* **c. Identify a failure or area of improvement in a submission, project, or team environment:**
  * *Accomplished (5 pts):* Failure or area of improvement is identified for a submission, project, or team environment.
  * *Developing/Inadequate (1–2 pts):* Failure or area of improvement is misidentified or not identified.

---

### Criteria 3.4: Test & Validate Solutions (EMLO 12: Test and Validate Solutions)
* **Definition:** Develop a process to verify the solution meets design requirements and validate results.

#### Sub-Criteria & Behavioral Indicators:
* **a. Execute a plan to test how well solutions meet design requirements:**
  * *Accomplished (5 pts):* Plan is fully executed and results provided for how well the solution meets design requirements.
  * *Intermediate (3 pts):* Plan is executed or partially executed, providing results for only some design requirements.
  * *Developing/Inadequate (1–2 pts):* No plan is executed.
* **b. Construct a plan to test how well the solution meets design requirements:**
  * *Accomplished (5 pts):* Plan is constructed to fully test the solution and verify design requirements.
  * *Intermediate (3 pts):* Plan is provided to test the solution but only verifies some requirements (or does not identify how verification occurs).
  * *Developing/Inadequate (1–2 pts):* No plan is provided.

## 5. Bullet Point Build-Up & Cumulative Progression Matrix

To help coding agents understand how score tiers build upon each other (from Developing to Accomplished), the rubric defines cumulative criteria progression across EMLOs:

### Pillar 1: Curiosity Progression
* **Innovation (EMLO 2 - Analyze Accepted Solutions):**
  * *Developing/Beginning:* Implemented an existing solution without research or identified changes that did not improve the solution.
  * *Intermediate:* Developed a project based on research identifying strengths/weaknesses or considered specific improvements without thorough independent research.
  * *Advanced:* Developed a project based on research identifying strengths/weaknesses and explored intentional improvements (form, fit, function) based on evidence.
  * *Accomplished (Expert):* Determined possible gaps/flaws supported by direct experimentation/research AND explored intentional improvements based on evidence identifying strengths/weaknesses, successfully creating a more successful solution.

* **Creativity (EMLO 1 - Demonstrate Curiosity):**
  * *Developing:* Did not ask questions addressing understanding.
  * *Intermediate:* Asked questions to better understand the area of interest / structured group inquiry attendance without active engagement.
  * *Advanced:* Formulated unique/new questions to bridge knowledge gaps and made a plan exploring answers to improve project outcomes.
  * *Accomplished (Expert):* Acted on a systematic plan to identify and close knowledge gaps, asking unique/new questions and executing artful applications of their knowledge.

### Pillar 2: Connections Progression
* **Communication & Visual Reps (EMLO 9 - Develop Concepts & Visual Representations):**
  * *Developing:* No prototype or visual representation presented.
  * *Intermediate:* Basic sketches, drawings, or diagrams representing some elements / basic visual representation explaining the value of modeling.
  * *Advanced:* Preliminary conceptual solutions illustrated via physical prototype and visual explanation.
  * *Accomplished (Expert):* Functional prototype of essential elements combined with detailed sketches, drawings, and diagrams based on research and iterative design processes.

* **Project Complexity (EMLO 3 - Integrate Information):**
  * *Developing:* Single idea from a single knowledge area; no connections.
  * *Intermediate:* Connects ideas within the same domain or from more than one technical area of knowledge.
  * *Advanced:* Connects ideas across different areas of humanities, engineering, and science (more than 2 sources).
  * *Accomplished (Expert):* Fully synthesized ideas from a wide variety of sources across humanities, science, and engineering to create something new, future-oriented, and effectively complex.

### Pillar 3: Creating Value Progression
* **Impact (EMLO 5 - Identify Opportunity to Create Value):**
  * *Developing:* Project did not seek to provide a solution with potential tangible value; no opportunity proposed.
  * *Intermediate:* Identified features of their solution with potential value / refined features using research.
  * *Advanced:* Proposed and justified a detailed concept with potential value, but not currently feasible for development.
  * *Accomplished (Expert):* Research-backed justification; proposed opportunity creates value AND can be developed through a feasible prototype.

* **Growth & Navigating Challenges (EMLO 6 - Learn from Failure):**
  * *Developing:* Failures or areas of improvement were not identified or reflected upon.
  * *Intermediate:* Identified failures/areas of improvement and considered alternative approaches.
  * *Advanced:* Identified failures, reflected on alternative approaches, and implemented alternatives based on identified lessons learned.
  * *Accomplished (Expert):* Consistent recording and reflection on personal/team failures resulting in successful pivots and implemented solutions, including documented lessons learned.

## 7. Automated Agent Self-Grading & Compliance Protocol

Before presenting final code, documentation, or architecture to the user, the coding agent **must** evaluate its output against this rubric checklist:

```markdown
### Agent Self-Audit Checklist (OHI/O Rubric Compliance)

1. [ ] **Innovation & Contrarian Analysis (EMLO 2)**: Does the README or code documentation explicitly state what existing solution was analyzed, what gaps/flaws were discovered, and how this project offers a novel alternative?
2. [ ] **Technical Complexity & Cross-Domain Synthesis (EMLO 3)**: Does the codebase integrate multiple frameworks, APIs, data sources, or domains (e.g., combining technical systems with real-world data/science) rather than a single trivial script?
